Outlook mail:

People's complaints about Microsoft Outlook center on the heavy, slow nature of Classic Outlook versus the stripped-down, web-app feel and missing enterprise features of New Outlook.

What People Hate About Classic Outlook

• Sluggish Performance: Slow startup times, high resource/RAM usage, and laggy search indexing.
• Bloated and Cluttered UI: The traditional ribbon interface can feel overwhelming and dated for users who just want a clean email client.
• Legacy Baggage: Heavy reliance on local .pst files, aging architecture, and vulnerability to crashing when syncing large Exchange or POP3 mailboxes.
• Poor Modern/Mobile Sync: Disjointed cross-device experience compared to lightweight web-first clients.

What People Hate About New Outlook

• Just a "Web App in a Box": It is essentially PWA (Progressive Web App) wrapper over Outlook on the web, lacking true native desktop responsiveness.
• Missing Power-User & Enterprise Features: Broken or absent support for classic COM add-ins, VSTO plugins, VBA macros, complex local PST management, shared mailboxes, and advanced custom rules.
• Inconsistent Offline Access: Limited offline capabilities make working without a stable internet connection frustrating compared to Classic Outlook.
• Aggressive Ads and Prompts: Free and even some paid users complain about banner ads appearing directly inside the message list.
• Buggy Behavior: Reports of unsent drafts sticking around, delayed search indexing, phantom account re-appearances, and missing signature settings.

How It Could Be Made Better

• Bridge Native Speed with Web Agility: Retain a true native desktop engine (or a high-performance Rust/C++ or hybrid core) rather than forcing a pure browser wrapper, fixing latency and offline parity.
• Restore Full Add-In and Automation Support: Provide seamless migration pathways or compatibility layers for legacy COM add-ins, macros, and third-party enterprise tools instead of deprecating them prematurely.
• True Local-First Offline Mode: Ensure offline .pst and local data handling work identically to Classic Outlook without requiring constant cloud handshakes.
• Clean Up the UI & Remove Inbox Ads: Separate promotional/ad content completely from user mailboxes and allow granular layout customization.
• Feature Parity Before Phasing Out: Stop pushing automated transitions until core workflows (advanced rules, multi-attachment handling, robust contact views, and local file hyperlinking) match or exceed Classic Outlook.

Apple Mail:

While Apple Mail is appreciated for its clean integration into the Apple ecosystem, privacy features, and lack of ads, it faces significant criticism from power users.
People primarily dislike Apple Mail because it lacks modern, AI-driven productivity features and suffers from persistent syncing and formatting bugs.

❌ Why People "Hate" Apple Mail

Critics and daily users generally point to a few long-standing frustrations:
• Outdated Search Functionality: The search bar is often slow and inaccurate. Users frequently report it failing to find specific text or older messages unless explicit, complex filters are applied.
• Bad Rich Text and Formatting: Composing emails often results in strange layout shifts. Copying and pasting text or images into a draft frequently breaks the formatting, making messages look messy on the recipient's end (especially if they use Outlook or Gmail).
• Attachment Clumsiness: Instead of keeping files neatly grouped at the bottom, Apple Mail forces images and PDFs to display "inline" (fully expanded inside the text), which disrupts the reading flow of long messages.
• Frequent Sync and Notification Delays: Users across macOS and iOS regularly complain about ghost unread notification badges, slow IMAP syncing, or the app failing to fetch new mail in the background without being manually opened.
• Basic Rules and Automation: The mail filtering "Rules" system is only available on the Mac app (not iOS) and feels like a relic from a decade ago. It lacks the smart, automated sorting capabilities of modern alternatives.

💡 Features Users Constantly Request

To bridge the gap between Apple Mail and third-party competitors (like Outlook, Spark, or Superhuman), users consistently ask for the following upgrades:

Core Functionality

• True Push Notifications for Gmail: Because of a long-standing protocol limitation between Apple and Google, Gmail accounts on Apple Mail rely on periodic "fetching" rather than instant push notifications.
• Unified Rules Across Ecosystem: The ability to create, edit, and sync mail rules and filters seamlessly across iPhone, iPad, and Mac.
• Improved Attachment Handling: A simple, global toggle to view all attachments as clean icons instead of forcing inline previews.

Modern Productivity

• Deeper Categorization: A native, automated sorting system similar to Gmail's "Primary, Social, Updates, Promotions" tabs to keep the main inbox clean.
• Smarter AI Integration: Richer implementation of Apple Intelligence to draft high-quality contextual replies, automatically surface urgent emails, and accurately surface old threads via natural language search.
• Snooze Customization: Better granular control over "Snooze" or "Remind Me" times so users can easily default to custom intervals (e.g., "Tomorrow at 9 AM" or "Next Weekend").
